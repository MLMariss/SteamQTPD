## Axis 2 — Refresh schedule (is the pipeline keeping up?)

Covered rows split by how each row's **own scraper** will treat it on the next pass — NOT against a flat target. The **7d (active)** lane is games updated within 90d (short cooldown); the **30d (dormant)** lane is everyone else (long cooldown, refreshed rarely *by design*). Track totals **include** their overdue members. **overdue** = already past its lane's cooldown = real backlog. **never** = no data yet = fill frontier. **empty** = correctly skipped (below the row's own review floor / null score), not pending work.

### Playtime raw — surface refresh (`playtime_raw/`)

Staleness is the **real per-game `scraped_at`**, measured against the real ladder (release-age tiers <7d→1d · <30d→3d · <90d→7d · else 30d; halved above 1,000 reviews, then floored by the popularity tiers >1000→5d/>500→10d). Because the cooldown varies per game, **promise** is the range actually applied within each release-age tier. `covered` 100,370 · `overdue` 13,320 · `never` 1 · `empty` 34,736.

| Release age | n | Promise | Median stale | p90 | Inside window |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0–7 d | 426 | 0.5–1 d | 1.7 d | 3.9 d | 31.2% |
| 7–30 d | 907 | 1.5–3 d | 3.7 d | 9.8 d | 39.6% |
| 30–90 d | 3,078 | 3.5–7 d | 6.1 d | 13.8 d | 61.7% |
| 90 d+ | 95,959 | 5–30 d | 9.1 d | 21.8 d | 88.2% |

**A surface refresh is not a full one.** For a game already holding 3,000 reviews a normal visit stops after page 1, so only its newest ~100 playtimes are re-read; positions below that stay frozen at whatever they were when first captured. Small games (below their rung) are walked to the end of Steam's list every visit, so for them this table *is* the whole story.

Files with a single-window (not two-track) refresh rule:

- **`games.json` core** (`scraper.py`): fresh 46,027 · overdue 89,080 · never 0 · last-modified-only 0. Games within 365d of release are on the age-tiered review refresh (≤3d→0.25d, ≤10d→0.5d, ≤30d→1d, ≤60d→2d, ≤90d→3.5d, ≤180d→7d, ≤365d→15d), so fresh/overdue are exact for them. Older games refresh on Steam's `last_modified` (invisible locally) and the PICS review-drift trigger, and at least every 60d (the floor, behind all other work), which is the window they are scored against here.
- **`hltb.json`** (`hltb_refresh.py`): fresh 35,155 · overdue 2 · blank-frozen 100,165 · blank-active 88. Windows: partial 14d, full 365d; blanks back off 3→30→180d by attempt count.

- **`tags.json`** (`tags_refresh.py`): **no timestamp stored and no rescrape schedule** — coverage-only. See Future work below.
- **`playtime.json` / `ratings.json`**: derived from `playtime_raw/` on every raw pass; staleness inherits from the raw shard row above.

## Notes

**Prices.** `prices.json` holds **115,870 rows (85.8% of catalog)**, tracking the non-free base (**115,880** games after removing 19,227 `is_free`). **14,461 of 115,870 priced titles (12.5%) are currently discounted**; sale end-dates come from the `IStoreBrowseService/GetItems` pass.

**Playtime backfill.** Raw playtime covers **100,393 games (74.3% of catalog)**. The honest denominator is the addressable set after the `MIN_REVIEWS_FLOOR = 5` gate — **100,371 games (74.3% of catalog)** — against which raw is **100.0% of addressable**. The other **34,736 games are below the floor and correctly skipped** (too few reviews to produce a sentiment-split median).

**Summarizers run on every raw pass.** Raw 100,393 · summarized 100,251 · rated 100,223 (current gap 142). `playtime_summarize.py` and `ratings_summarize.py` are chained into `playtime-raw.yml`, so the small frontend files refresh right after the shards do (8×/day). Any residual gap is just within-run ordering between the shard commit and the summarize steps, not a stalled pipeline.

**Update events.** `updates.json` (event_type-based patch history) covers **55,585 games (41.1%)**, built from **64 of 64 `updates_raw/` shards populated**. This layer feeds the frontend's **Updated column cadence badge** (`N · 90d` / `N · 1y`, from the summed 90d/365d `counts`) and backfills a null News-API `last_update_ts`. It is gated by its own 10-review floor (playtime's is 5; the two no longer match). As the remaining shards populate this coverage rises; the precedence flip (event layer becomes primary over News-API) is gated on that coverage — see ARCHITECTURE §9.5 / §3.1.

**HLTB estimates refresh live.** **19,213 of 135,410 `hltb.json` entries carry the `est` flag** (14.2%), leaving **116,197 reals (86.0%)**. `hltb_estimate.py` is a shared helper imported by `hltb_refresh.py` (as `HE`) and called in the fill loop, so estimates recompute from live median ratios every 2h as new reals land — not a stale one-off.

**Rating % / review count.** Rating % is present for **121,895 games (90.2%)**; review count for **125,630 (93.0%)**. The gap is titles with too few reviews to carry a meaningful score.

**Tags** are non-empty for **135,187 games (100.1%)**. The remainder are untagged on SteamSpy (typically very-low-review or unreleased titles).

**Upcoming catalogue** (`upcoming.json`, ARCHITECTURE §6 step 3): **61,601 of 61,697 pending games have a row (99.8%)** — 4,471 with a day-precision date, 1,714 adult by store descriptors — and **61,582** have PICS records in `pics_upcoming.json`. `upcoming_refresh.py` (2.11) refreshes every pending game daily in one batched pass.

**Demos** (`demos.json`, `demos.py`): **39,664 games list a demo** — 21,463 released, 18,201 upcoming. Last full sweep: 2026-11-01 08:56.

## Future work

- **Tag refresh schedule.** `tags.json` currently has no per-entry timestamp and no rescrape cadence — tags are fetched once and left. Tags do drift (Steam user-tag votes shift, new tags get added), so a light periodic re-check (e.g. weekly/monthly, oldest-first, small budget) is worth adding. That needs a per-entry `scraped_at` in `tags.json` first, after which tags can join Axis 2 as a proper row here.
- **Uniform-refresh review.** Once the fill frontier drains (near-100% coverage), revisit whether the dormant 30–45d cooldowns should tighten toward a flatter, faster whole-catalog refresh. Today's split (active 4–7d / dormant 30–45d) deliberately front-loads budget onto games whose data actually moves; a uniform target only makes sense once no backlog is competing for that budget.
